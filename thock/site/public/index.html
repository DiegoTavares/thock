Why it exists

Second brains already exist. None of them adapt to your taste.

People build this by hand — a vault of plain-text notes, a fast editor, an AI that reads the week and helps close it. It works, and it never transfers, because it's held together by conventions only its author understands.

The ones you can install have the opposite problem. They arrive finished: someone else's folder names, someone else's rituals, someone else's idea of what a good week looks like. You adapt to the tool, and it never adapts back.

Thock is neither, because there is nothing in it that isn't a file. The rituals, the folder structure, the templates, the theme, the font, the keybindings — all of it sits in your vault in plain text. Ask your agent for a new Routine, a shorter weekly review, a quieter colour scheme, and it edits those files the way it would edit any others. Nothing is compiled in. Nothing is hidden from it.

  1. Your files, forever, in the open. plain Markdown in a normal folder · no database, no lock-in · if Thock disappeared tomorrow, your vault opens in any editor
  2. It appends. It never rewrites. synthesis lands under its own heading, like # Daily Closure · what you wrote stays exactly as you wrote it
  3. Bring your own brain. your own LLM, your own key: Claude, a local model, whatever you trust · Thock doesn't resell intelligence
  4. You act. It only advises. it computes exactly what to do next, but it cannot and will not do the important things for you · money moves when you move it
  5. History without the vocabulary. every meaningful change checkpointed in the background · restore any version of any note · nothing is ever called a commit
  6. Everything is editable, by you or your agent. rituals, templates, layouts, theme, font, keybindings — all files in your vault · rewrite them yourself, or just ask

Routines

Install a rhythm, not an app full of features

A Routine is one life-domain, packaged: its folders, templates, quick links and rituals. Enable the ones you want; ignore the rest. Each is defined by a readable file in your vault, so your own agent can author a new one from scratch.

routines/<id>/routine.toml One file describes a whole Routine — its folders, templates, links and rituals.

Add one, change one, or delete one, and nothing else in your vault notices.

The editor

Built on the bones of a fast code editor, without the face of one

Thock inherits its editing core from Zed: native, instant, and happy in a ten-thousand-file folder. The code-editor chrome is gone: no diagnostics, no debugger, no source-control pane. What's left is a writing instrument.

Markdown that reads like a page. While your cursor is elsewhere, heading markers and link syntax fold away, checkboxes draw as checkboxes, and wikilinks are visibly links. The line you're editing always shows its exact source. The file on disk never changes.

Keyboard, everywhere. Every panel is fully operable without the mouse: arrows or vim motions, your choice. Every action has a name and a binding. This page works the same way: press ?.

Removed from Zed diagnostics · debugger · source-control pane · subscription upsells

Kept the editing core, and its speed

Your vault

Already keep a vault? Point Thock at it.

A Thock vault is a normal folder of Markdown, which means the one you already have probably is one. No import, no conversion, no migration. [[wikilinks]] resolve, your folder structure is respected, and everything Thock adds is a readable file sitting beside your notes.

Open the same folder in your current tools whenever you like. Nothing breaks, because nothing changed shape.

Who it's for

Opinionated and personal, by design

Probably for you if

  • You already think in plain text and live on the keyboard.
  • You want AI woven into your life-admin (reviews, finances, journaling) without hand-building the plumbing.
  • You've tried to wire an editor, a vault, and an LLM together yourself, and kept the scars.
  • You want your notes readable in thirty years, by anything.

Probably not, if

  • You're looking for a team wiki or a shared workspace.
  • You want an AI that acts on your behalf. Thock advises; you act.
  • You'd rather have a database app with forms and views than a folder of files.
