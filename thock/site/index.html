Why it exists

People already build this by hand. It just doesn't transfer.

The setup works: a vault of plain-text notes, a fast editor, an AI that reads your week and helps you close it. But today it's held together by conventions, prompt files, and folder discipline that only its author understands. Powerful, and completely non-transferable.

Thock ships that opinion as a product. The folder structure, the rituals, the layouts, and the guardrails are visible, editable features instead of tribal knowledge.

Your files, forever, in the open

Plain Markdown in a normal folder. No database, no lock-in. If Thock disappeared tomorrow, your vault opens in any editor.

Appends, never rewrites

The AI adds its synthesis under headings like # Daily Closure. What you wrote stays exactly as you wrote it.

Bring your own brain

You connect your own LLM with your own key: Claude, a local model, whatever you trust. Thock doesn't resell intelligence.

You act, it advises

It computes exactly what to do next, but it cannot and will not do the important things for you. Money moves when you move it.

Invisible history

Every meaningful change is checkpointed in the background. Restore any version of any note, with no source-control vocabulary anywhere.

Everything is editable

Rituals, templates and layouts are files in your vault. Rewrite them yourself, or ask your agent to.

Routines

Install a rhythm, not an app full of features

A Routine is one life-domain, packaged: its folders, templates, quick links and rituals. Enable the ones you want; ignore the rest. Each is defined by a readable file in your vault, so your own agent can author a new one from scratch.

Timeline: days and weeks

Today, Yesterday, This Week, Last Week. One keystroke each, filenames resolved for you. Wrap rituals read the day's tasks and commits and append an honest closure. A week review gathers it all every Friday.

Backlog: nothing dies in yesterday's note

A plain backlog.md with Soon, Someday, and a dated Completed history. When you wrap a day, unfinished tasks are offered a place there; you confirm all, none, or some. Checking one off records it in today's note.

Inbox: capture anywhere, decide at the desk

A folder anything can drop a note into: a link shared from your phone, an email you label, a file dragged in. A triage ritual proposes a destination for each item and files only what you confirm. Capture is dumb and instant; deciding happens where you think.

phone share sheet · email label · plain files in a folder

Skills: rituals you can read

Every ritual is an inspectable Markdown file with a plain-language description and declared scopes: what it reads, what it writes, and whether it appends or edits. Trust comes from being able to open the thing and read it.

The editor

Built on the bones of a fast code editor, without the face of one

Thock inherits its editing core from Zed: native, instant, and happy in a ten-thousand-file folder. The code-editor chrome is gone: no diagnostics, no debugger, no source-control pane. What's left is a writing instrument.

Markdown that reads like a page. While your cursor is elsewhere, heading markers and link syntax fold away, checkboxes draw as checkboxes, and wikilinks are visibly links. The line you're editing always shows its exact source. The file on disk never changes.

Keyboard, everywhere. Every panel is fully operable without the mouse: arrows or vim motions, your choice. Every action has a name and a binding. This page works the same way: press ?.

Your vault

Already keep a vault? Point Thock at it.

A Thock vault is a normal folder of Markdown, which means the one you already have probably is one. No import, no conversion, no migration. [[wikilinks]] resolve, your folder structure is respected, and everything Thock adds is a readable file sitting beside your notes.

Open the same folder in your current tools whenever you like. Nothing breaks, because nothing changed shape.

Who it's for

Opinionated and personal, by design

Probably for you if

  • You already think in plain text and live on the keyboard.
  • You want AI woven into your life-admin (reviews, finances, journaling) without hand-building the plumbing.
  • You've tried to wire an editor, a vault, and an LLM together yourself, and kept the scars.
  • You want your notes readable in thirty years, by anything.

Probably not, if

  • You're looking for a team wiki or a shared workspace.
  • You want an AI that acts on your behalf. Thock advises; you act.
  • You'd rather have a database app with forms and views than a folder of files.
